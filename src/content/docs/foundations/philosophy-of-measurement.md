**[Overview slides](/measuring-self-reports/slides/tal-chang.pdf)** (PDF)

Metrology says what a measurement consists of. The philosophy of measurement asks how any of it can be trusted when there is no independent access to the quantity measured: a thermometer can only be checked against other thermometers, and a theory of heat can only be tested with readings from them. The problem is old, and it is the situation of every introspection experiment on a large language model (LLM), where no instrument for reading internal states is already trusted.

Three bodies of work on this site answer it in different ways:

| Page | The answer | What it adds to the card |
|---|---|---|
| [Model-based measurement (Tal)](/measuring-self-reports/foundations/tal/) | An outcome is inferred from an indication through a model of the measurement process; calibration is building and testing that model, and trust comes from coherence across a network of instruments | The indication/outcome distinction; black-box, white-box and coherent calibration; when two instruments measure the same quantity |
| [Epistemic iteration (Chang)](/measuring-self-reports/foundations/chang/) | Start from a standard accepted without proof and correct it through the inquiries it makes possible | Comparability and the principle of single value; carrying a measurement into a new domain |
| [Robustness and convergence](/measuring-self-reports/foundations/robustness/) | Agreement among independent procedures is evidence that a result is real, provided the procedures really are independent | Illusory robustness from shared methods; convergence versus calibration against a preferred method |

The three answers agree that no measurement is justified from a foundation outside all theory. They differ on what does the justifying: the model of the process (Tal), the history of corrections (Chang), or the independence of the procedures (robustness). An introspection experiment can be assessed against each: whether it models how the report is produced, whether its standard has been revised in light of what the reports show, and whether the procedures it cites as agreeing are independent of each other.
