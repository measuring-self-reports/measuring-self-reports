**Source.** *Introspective Coupling: Self-Explanation Training Tracks Behavioral Change Despite Fixed Supervision* ([Guo, Ruis, Andreas & Li, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32038)), MIT, arXiv preprint. Not peer reviewed. No code or label release is stated.

**Description.** Guo, Ruis, Andreas and Li train language models to answer a counterfactual question about their own behavior: if a cue were removed from the input, would the answer change, and to what? The training labels are built once from the base model's behavior and never refreshed. Training shifts the model's behavior, so on some items the labels and the model's current behavior come apart, and the paper asks which of the two the trained model's explanations match. A better match to current behavior is called **Self > Orig** and read as **introspective coupling**. Models: Qwen3-8B (main), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-32B.

## Measurement card

| Stage | What the paper reports |
|---|---|
| Measurand | Defined operationally: a model shows Self > Orig if its explanations predict its current behavior better than the behavior its training labels came from. Introspective coupling is the model tying its explanations to its current behavior rather than reproducing fixed targets. The score is exact match against two label sets. |
| Procedure | The training objective (explanation loss plus a KL term that keeps behavior near the base model), three tasks and their cues, learning rate and fine-tuning settings are given. The explanation question contains the original input. |
| Instrument and indication | The trained model's templated answer: whether the response would change, and to what. Read as exact match, with per-class F1 reported separately. |
| Measurement model | Exact match is split into items where base and current labels agree and items where they disagree; the Self − Orig gap comes only from the second group. Per-class F1 rules out a model that always gives one answer. |
| Calibration | The reference for Self is the trained model's own counterfactual behavior, rolled out after training; for Orig it is the base model's behavior. For Refusal the behavior label is scored by an LLM judge. Datasets are named; no code or labels are released. |
| Uncertainty | The KL weight (five orders of magnitude), online label agreement, label mixing, LoRA rank, learning rate, three models, three tasks and four auxiliary corpora are varied. The gap is absent with no KL term and at low LoRA rank. No seeds, repeated runs or error bars. |
| Comparability | An external explainer trained on the trained model's labels is compared with the model explaining itself. An untrained few-shot base model is a baseline. Activation patching gives a second, mechanistic reading. |
| Validity conditions | Held-out items within each task, out-of-domain nonsense ("Jabberwocky") items whose behavior cannot come from the base model, and four auxiliary corpora. The paper states that training needs behavioral variation between the two inputs, and that its account of when coupling emerges is incomplete. |

## Design

1. **Base behavior.** The base model answers each input with and without a cue. Three tasks: Hint-MMLU (an injected "Hint: A"), AITA (Reddit posts framed in first or third person) and Refusal (prompts with generated role system prompts).
2. **Labels.** From each pair of answers, a templated label: "The response [would/would not] change to <M(x\C)>", where M(x\C) is the answer without the cue.
3. **Training.** Fine-tuning on the labels, plus a KL term with weight λ keeping behavior near the base model. λ = 1 gives the regularized model; λ = 0 the unregularized one.
4. **Relabeling.** After training, the trained model's own behavior is rolled out and new labels built from it.
5. **Scoring.** The model's explanations are scored by exact match against the original labels (Orig) and against its own (Self).
6. **Mechanism.** Activation patching on Hint-MMLU, comparing effects on the behavior and on the explanation at each layer and token.
7. **Sweeps.** KL weight, online label agreement, mixing in labels from Llama, LoRA rank and learning rate.
8. **Generalization.** Behavior-only training on nonsense questions and other corpora alongside explanation training.

## Results

- Where the original labels and the model's own behavior disagree, the regularized model sides with its own behavior 79%, 82% and 63% of the time for Hint-MMLU, AITA and Refusal. Without regularization the order reverses.
- Patching effects on behavior and explanation correlate at r = 0.89 for the regularized model, 0.527 for the unregularized one and 0.20 for the base model.
- The Self > Orig gap appears for any KL weight above about 5 × 10⁻³.
- With online relabeling, coupling holds when at least 70% of labels agree with the model's own, and collapses below 60%.
- Qwen and Llama labels agree on only 53% of items; Self > Orig holds at every mix, while Self accuracy falls from 88% to 69% as Llama labels replace Qwen's.
- A self-explainer beats an external explainer trained on the same labels.
- The few-shot base model reaches only 14–18% exact match.
- After behavior-only training on nonsense items, the model explains them at 90.5%, and 79.8% on held-out nonsense items, without explanation supervision.
- The gap is absent at LoRA rank 80 or below and opens at 96. The learning-rate sweep runs against the paper's main hypothesis: the gap grows as the model drifts further from the base.
